# 1. Purpose

The AI Safety & Guardrails Architecture defines how PesoPilot protects users, safeguards financial intelligence workflows, enforces responsible AI behavior, and validates interactions across the entire AI Platform.

Unlike other AI Platform subsystems, AI Safety is not an isolated execution engine.

It is a cross-cutting architectural capability that influences every stage of AI execution.

Its responsibilities include:

* validating user requests,
* protecting user privacy,
* preventing unsafe prompt construction,
* enforcing financial guidance boundaries,
* validating AI responses,
* protecting memory integrity,
* governing provider interactions,
* ensuring explainable AI behavior.

The objective is to ensure that every AI workflow remains safe, deterministic, auditable, and trustworthy.

# 3. AI Safety Philosophy

PesoPilot adopts one guiding principle.

> **Every AI interaction must be validated before execution and verified before publication.**

AI safety is preventive rather than reactive.

Instead of attempting to repair unsafe behavior after generation, the platform minimizes unsafe situations before they occur.

Safety is therefore integrated into every execution workflow rather than existing as a standalone validation step.

---

# 4. Guardrail Philosophy

The Guardrail Engine acts as the policy enforcement layer of the AI Platform.

Policies define **what** is permitted.

The Guardrail Engine determines **whether** an operation complies with those policies.

It never performs:

* financial calculations,
* prompt engineering,
* memory management,
* provider execution,
* conversation management.

Its sole responsibility is enforcing AI governance consistently across all AI subsystems.

---

# 5. AI Safety Principles

The AI Platform follows ten architectural safety principles.

---

## 5.1 Default Safe Behavior

When uncertainty exists, the safest valid outcome takes precedence over the most permissive outcome.

---

## 5.2 Least Privilege

Every AI component receives only the information necessary to complete the current workflow.

No subsystem receives unrestricted platform access.

---

## 5.3 Explainability

Every safety decision must be explainable.

Safety validations should produce diagnostics describing:

* evaluated rule,
* outcome,
* reason,
* timestamp.

---

## 5.4 Provider Independence

Safety logic must never depend on a specific LLM provider.

Changing providers must not change platform safety behavior.

---

## 5.5 Deterministic Enforcement

Identical inputs evaluated under identical policies must always produce identical safety decisions.

---

## 5.6 Layered Protection

Multiple independent guardrails protect every workflow.

No single validation mechanism should become a single point of failure.

---

## 5.7 Privacy by Design

Sensitive information should remain protected throughout every stage of AI execution.

Privacy enforcement is built into the architecture rather than added later.

---

## 5.8 Financial Responsibility

AI assists financial decision-making.

AI never replaces user judgment or licensed professional advice.

Generated guidance must remain educational and decision-support oriented.

---

## 5.9 Auditability

Every rejected request, overridden policy, validation failure, and safety decision must remain observable through diagnostics and audit logs.

---

## 5.10 Extensibility

Safety policies must evolve without requiring architectural redesign.

New guardrails should be added through policy expansion rather than subsystem modification.

---

# 9. Guardrail Engine Overview

The Guardrail Engine is the centralized enforcement subsystem responsible for applying every AI safety policy.

```text
Guardrail Engine

├── Input Validator

├── Prompt Validator

├── Memory Validator

├── Provider Validator

├── Response Validator

├── Financial Guidance Validator

├── Privacy Manager

├── Prompt Injection Detector

├── Abuse Detector

├── Audit Logger

├── Policy Manager

├── Safety Diagnostics

└── Version
```

The Guardrail Engine does not generate AI responses.

It evaluates every interaction against platform policies and either:

* approves execution,
* modifies execution where policy allows,
* rejects execution,
* escalates execution,
* records diagnostics.

It is therefore the single enforcement point for AI governance across the entire PesoPilot AI Platform.

The purpose of Input Validation is to ensure that only structurally valid, policy-compliant, and safe requests enter the AI Platform.

The Guardrail Engine validates:

* request schema,
* required fields,
* request size,
* supported workflow type,
* authentication context,
* authorization context,
* rate limits,
* malformed payloads.

Input Validation executes before the AI Orchestration Engine creates a workflow.

# 16. Financial Advice Guardrails

PesoPilot provides financial coaching.

It does not provide regulated financial advice.

The Financial Guidance Validator ensures responses remain within supported boundaries.

Approved guidance includes:

* budgeting,
* savings planning,
* expense analysis,
* financial education,
* cashflow interpretation,
* goal tracking,
* spending insights,
* financial habit coaching.

The AI must avoid presenting speculative investment recommendations, legal conclusions, tax advice, or guarantees of financial outcomes as authoritative facts.

Responses should communicate uncertainty when appropriate and encourage users to consult qualified professionals for decisions that require regulated expertise.

```text

↓

↓

```

This layer preserves user trust while reducing the risk of inappropriate financial guidance.

# 17. Hallucination Prevention

Hallucinations reduce confidence in AI-generated insights.

The AI Platform minimizes hallucinations through layered validation rather than relying solely on model behavior.

The Guardrail Engine applies several defensive strategies.

## 17.1 Grounded Responses

Whenever possible, AI responses should be grounded in verified platform data.

Examples include:

* InsightBundle
* Rule Engine outputs
* Summary Engine outputs
* Health Scores
* Recommendation Bundles
* User-approved Memory Context

The AI should explain these facts rather than invent new ones.

---

## 17.2 Deterministic Financial Data

The AI must never fabricate:

* balances,
* expenses,
* income,
* savings,
* goals,
* Health Scores,
* Recommendation Scores,
* calculated metrics.

Every numerical statement should originate from deterministic platform calculations.

---

## 17.3 Unsupported Claims

If sufficient information is unavailable, the preferred response is uncertainty.

Examples include:

* requesting clarification,
* explaining missing information,
* identifying unavailable data.

The AI should never invent facts to complete an answer.

---

## 17.4 Context Boundaries

The Prompt Builder injects only:

* Conversation Context,
* Workflow Context,
* Memory Context,
* deterministic InsightBundle data.

Information outside these boundaries should not influence generated responses.

---

## 17.5 Explainability

Whenever practical, significant recommendations should be explainable through supporting platform evidence.

Examples include:

```text
Recommendation

↓

Supporting Rules

↓

Supporting Insights

↓

Financial Summary

↓

User Response
```

This improves transparency and user confidence.

---

## 17.6 Safety Escalation

If the Guardrail Engine determines that a response cannot be validated with sufficient confidence, it may:

* reject publication,
* request clarification,
* trigger regeneration,
* escalate to manual review (future capability).

Safety always takes precedence over response completeness.

# 25. Architecture Decision Records

## ADR-336

### Why Introduce a Dedicated Guardrail Engine?

**Decision**

AI safety policies are enforced by a centralized Guardrail Engine.

**Reason**

Provides a single enforcement point, eliminates duplicated validation logic, and ensures consistent behavior across every AI subsystem.

---

## ADR-337

### Why Treat Safety as a Cross-Cutting Architecture Concern?

**Decision**

AI Safety influences every stage of AI execution instead of existing as a standalone subsystem.

**Reason**

Security, privacy, and governance must protect the complete workflow rather than isolated components.

---

## ADR-338

### Why Validate Both Requests and Responses?

**Decision**

Safety validation occurs before provider execution and again before response publication.

**Reason**

Prevents unsafe inputs from reaching language models while ensuring unsafe outputs never reach users.

---

## ADR-339

### Why Separate Policy Definition from Policy Enforcement?

**Decision**

Policies define acceptable behavior, while the Guardrail Engine enforces them.

**Reason**

Separates governance from implementation, making policies easier to evolve without redesigning the architecture.

---

## ADR-340

### Why Prioritize Explainability?

**Decision**

Every significant safety decision produces diagnostics and audit events.

**Reason**

Improves transparency, debugging, operational monitoring, regulatory readiness, and user trust.

# Document Summary

Document 12.6 establishes the AI Safety & Guardrails Architecture as the governance and protection layer of the PesoPilot AI Platform. Through a centralized Guardrail Engine, layered safety validation, deterministic policy enforcement, privacy protection, prompt injection defense, financial guidance boundaries, response validation, and comprehensive audit logging, the architecture ensures that every AI workflow operates within clearly defined safety constraints. Rather than relying on individual subsystems to implement their own protections, the Guardrail Engine provides a unified enforcement mechanism that preserves clean architecture while enabling explainable, trustworthy, and enterprise-ready AI behavior. This design prepares PesoPilot for future regulatory requirements, advanced AI capabilities, and multi-provider deployments without compromising user privacy, platform integrity, or architectural consistency.
